Write-up draftedEvidence strong

We used Anomalib's PatchCore, which learns only from good blocks. Anything that looks unlike the good examples scores high. Over three rounds of tests we picked a camera, then tightened the photo until the model could see a dot a few pixels across.

How PatchCore decides

A pretrained network (Wide ResNet-50) turns each small patch of a photo into a feature vector. From the good training photos PatchCore keeps a memory bank of these vectors. A new photo's score is how far its strangest patch lies from its nearest neighbour in the bank. No defect examples are needed for training, which suited us: we had plenty of good blocks and very few deliberately damaged ones.

The final model (Test 3)

Every photo is taken with the arm parked at the same pose, then cropped to a fixed 54 × 49 pixel box around the block (see Photo trigger & crop). The model trained on 29 good crops across 5 block colours and was tested on 8 good and 16 defective blocks it had never seen.

Threshold 0.37 is the midpoint between the highest good score (0.248) and the lowest visible-defect score (0.500). We didn't use the model's built-in 0.5: one defect scores exactly 0.500, and Anomalib's rule is strictly "greater than", so it would have called that block good.

One defect is invisible to this camera. ep33 scores 0: its mark is on a face the side camera can't see, even uncropped. Turning the block four times is meant to catch exactly this case.

One training photo was hurting. ep05, a "good" yellow block, had a small dark dot on it, which taught the model that small dots are normal. Removing it doubled the gap (0.127 → 0.252), and the smallest-dot defect's score rose from 0.50 to 0.73.

Limits we know about

  • The threshold was chosen on the test photos. The same 24 photos set the threshold and measured the result, so expect slightly worse separation on new blocks.
  • Small numbers. 29 training photos and 24 test photos. A gap of 0.25 is moderate, not large.
  • Speed not measured on the robot. The final model ran at about 110 ms per photo on the training laptop's integrated GPU. An earlier model ran at 3.8 ms on the robot laptop's Xe3 GPU, but the final model was never benchmarked there.
  • Fixed geometry. The crop is measured for this camera position and block spot. Move either and it must be re-measured.

Research we did not adopt

We studied how to make tiny dots easier to see at 320 × 240: averaging several frames, band-pass filtering, upscaling the crop. The main finding was that contrast stretching (CLAHE, histogram equalisation) makes dots harder to find, because it amplifies noise as much as the dot. These ideas were only tested on synthetic images, and the simpler tight crop was enough.
