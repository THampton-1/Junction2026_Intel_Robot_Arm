Adapt: a robot arm that inspects blocks for defects

At Junction 2026 our team of four had about two days to build a closed-loop inspection cell on Intel hardware: an SO-101 arm turns a Duplo block, a camera checks each face for defects, and the arm sorts the block into a good bowl or a defect bowl. We built and tested each part. We did not get the full loop running on the robot before the hardware failed.

The loop we set out to build

1 · PerceiveThe arm parks; the side camera photographs one face of the block.
→
2 · DetectAn Anomalib PatchCore model scores a tight crop of the photo. Score ≥ 0.37 means defect.
→
3 · RotateAn ACT policy grasps the block, turns it 90° and returns. Repeat for all four faces.
→
4 · SortAny defect: an ACT policy places it in the pink bowl. Otherwise the green bowl.

Every model was meant to run locally through OpenVINO on an Intel Core Ultra laptop, using its CPU, integrated GPU and NPU.

The story

Setup

Robot laptop, two arms, three cameras, three Python environments and a cloud trainer, all working together.

Defect detection

Three rounds of Anomalib tests. The side camera won, and a tight crop let the model see small dots.

Arm motion

Imitation learning (ACT) from teleoperated demonstrations: one policy to rotate the block, two to place it.

Photo trigger & crop

When to take the photo and which pixels to keep, measured on real recordings.

What happened

Joining the parts failed. An AI agent did the integration without a tested plan, and the arm's power supply browned out.
